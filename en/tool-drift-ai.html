Tool Overview

Drift is a US conversational marketing platform company founded in 2015, headquartered in Boston. Its core product is an AI-powered website chatbot and marketing automation platform that helps businesses turn website visitors into sales leads and customers. Drift's core philosophy is "replace forms with conversations" — traditional B2B sales processes require prospects to fill out lengthy contact forms and wait for a sales rep to reply, while Drift uses AI bots to converse with website visitors instantly, understand their needs, automatically schedule meetings or route them to the most suitable salesperson, dramatically shortening the "visitor to customer" conversion cycle.

In 2024, Drift was acquired by sales technology company Salesloft, and the integration formed a complete B2B sales technology stack covering "first touch to closed deal". Salesloft focuses on email sequences, call dialing and sales cadence management for sales teams, while Drift adds the front-end stage of "pre-sales conversation and lead conversion". The acquisition also means Drift's brand and products are gradually merging into the Salesloft platform. As of 2026, Drift (with its integrated Salesloft AI Chat feature) is used by well-known companies such as Adobe, LinkedIn and Okta, serving more than 5,000 B2B customers.

Core Features

1. AI-powered website chatbot: This is Drift's most fundamental and core feature. After a company embeds Drift's JavaScript code on its website, a chat window appears in the bottom-right corner. Drift's AI bot proactively starts conversations with visitors — not a simple "How can I help you?", but a personalized opening based on the page the visitor is viewing, the traffic source and past behavior (if they have visited before). For example, a visitor who arrives via a Google search for "enterprise CRM software" might be asked "Looking for a CRM solution? I can quickly compare a few leading products for you." The AI understands visitors' natural language replies, identifies purchase intent, budget range and decision-making role, then pushes high-quality sales leads directly to the most suitable sales rep.

2. Smart lead routing and meeting scheduling: When the AI determines that a visitor has clear purchase intent, it doesn't simply "transfer to a human" — it first collects key information (company name, job title, needs description), then automatically checks sales team members' calendar availability and schedules a mutually convenient call or video meeting between the visitor and a sales rep. The whole process happens inside the chat window — the visitor never leaves the page and doesn't wait for email confirmations. For sales teams, this means they receive "warm leads" — prospects already filtered by AI, with confirmed interest and even a meeting already booked.

3. Drift Prospector for proactive acquisition: This feature uses a company's existing customer data (such as CRM contacts and behavioral data) to identify "high-value accounts most likely to buy". It analyzes which website visitors come from target companies (via IP and domain matching) and automatically shows them customized chat content. Prospector also analyzes the common characteristics of historically won customers and recommends lookalike prospect segments in return.

4. Conversation Intelligence: Drift has a built-in conversation analysis engine that automatically performs sentiment analysis, intent classification and key topic extraction on every chat transcript. Managers can see data in the dashboard such as "the 3 most frequently asked questions across all conversations this week", "average time from chat to closed deal", and "chat satisfaction scores for different sales reps". These insights help companies continuously optimize chatbot scripts and sales communication strategies.

Getting Started

Drift is a typical B2B SaaS product; getting started usually involves support from the sales team and a Customer Success Manager (CSM). A typical onboarding flow: first discuss with Drift's sales team to assess team size and use cases, then choose a plan. After signing, a CSM helps complete the initial setup — including embedding Drift's tracking code on the company website (usually just adding a snippet of JS to the HTML, done in minutes), configuring the AI bot's conversation flows and scripts, and connecting CRM systems (Salesforce, HubSpot, etc.) to sync customer data.

The admin interface is a feature-rich console. The left navigation is divided into modules such as "Conversations", "Visitors", "Reports" and "Settings". In the Conversations view, sales reps see a live chat queue — the AI bot labels each visitor with an intent score and key information, and human operators decide whether to let the AI continue or jump in. The Visitors view shows a list of people currently browsing the website and their behavioral paths (which page they're viewing, where they spend the most time), like a "live visitor map".

Drift's learning curve lies in "conversation flow design" — to make the AI bot converse effectively with visitors, admins need to design "Playbooks" (conversation scripts) in the backend that define what the AI should say in different scenarios, what questions to ask, and how to judge intent levels. Drift offers a template library and AI-assisted suggestions, but designing a truly effective conversation flow still requires marketing and communication skills. Setting up a basic usable chatbot for the first time usually takes 1–2 days.

Pros and Limitations

Pros: First, the AI chatbot's conversational naturalness and lead-identification ability lead the B2B space — Drift has worked this niche for a decade, accumulating huge amounts of conversation data and scenario experience. Second, integrations with mainstream CRMs such as Salesforce and HubSpot are mature — online and offline data syncs and flows smoothly, reducing manual data entry. Third, real-time visitor monitoring gives sales teams the ability to "proactively reach out" — when a key target account is browsing the website, they can initiate a conversation.

Limitations: First, pricing is high — starting at $2,500/month, largely excluding SMBs. Second, deep dependence on the Salesloft ecosystem — since the acquisition, Drift's features are gradually merging into the Salesloft platform, and the standalone value of Drift is expected to keep declining. Third, AI conversation quality depends heavily on Playbook design — if the marketing team doesn't invest enough effort in designing conversation flows, the AI bot may perform worse than a simple "Contact Us" form.

FAQ

Q: Does Drift still have a standalone product after being acquired by Salesloft?
A: Drift's chatbot features have been gradually integrated into the Salesloft platform, positioned as "Salesloft AI Chat". The original Drift brand and product still operate, but in the long run, new customers are more likely to access these features through the Salesloft platform. We recommend following Salesloft's official site for the latest product news.

Q: Is Drift suitable for SMBs?
A: From a pricing perspective, Drift (from $2,500/month) is usually unaffordable for SMBs with annual revenue under $1 million. Alternatives like Intercom (from $39/month) or HubSpot's free chat tool may be more appropriate.

Q: Can the AI bot fully replace human customer support?
A: Drift's AI bot excels at "identifying intent, qualifying leads and scheduling meetings" — the front end of the pre-sales stage. In areas requiring in-depth product consultation, negotiation or after-sales service, human involvement is still essential. Drift's design philosophy is a human-machine collaboration model where "AI handles screening and routing, humans handle deep conversations".

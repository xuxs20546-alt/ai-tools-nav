📖 Overview

Perplexity is the flagship AI search engine that has redefined what "search" means. Traditional Google search gives you 10 blue links — you need to click through, read, and synthesize yourself. Perplexity gives you a structured answer cross-verified against multiple sources, with clear citation markers. It doesn't just answer — every sentence is backed by a [number] citation that links directly to the original webpage, letting you judge the information's reliability for yourself. This "verifiable" experience is Perplexity's biggest differentiator over ChatGPT.

Perplexity combines multiple large models under the hood (GPT-4, Claude, its own Sonar model, etc.), and in Pro Search mode it conducts multi-round searches — the AI searches and comprehends initial results, then automatically generates follow-up search queries, drilling deeper like a human researcher. The Pro tier also allows uploading files (PDFs, documents, etc.) for analysis within Perplexity, and you can use Collections to build thematic research and team-shared knowledge spaces. The free tier is sufficient for daily use, while Pro ($20/month) unlocks Pro Search (deeper, more citations), file uploads, and model selection.

✨ Pros & Cons

✅ Pros

  • Sentence-level citations, fully traceable: Not AI talking to itself — every factual statement comes with a linked source. Information credibility and transparency far exceed other AI products.
  • Pro Search multi-round deep search: AI automatically generates follow-up queries, drilling deeper layer by layer — this "recursive search" is extremely valuable for research questions requiring multi-angle understanding.
  • Clean interface, fast response: No ads, no SEO farm content. Every search yields precise answers rather than spammy farm pages.

⚠️ Cons

  • Citations sometimes from low-quality sources: AI cannot judge the authority of cited pages — it may reference a self-media article rather than an authoritative academic paper.
  • Chinese-language search quality lags behind English: The quality of Chinese internet information is uneven, and Perplexity's indexing coverage of Chinese websites is weaker than for English sites.
  • Cannot replace real-time information needs: For localized real-time queries like "today's weather" or "nearby restaurants," traditional search engines are still faster and more accurate.

🎯 Use Cases

  • In-depth research queries: "The changing landscape of AI chip supply chains in 2024" — for questions requiring multi-source synthesis, Perplexity is 10x faster than Google + manual reading.
  • Fact-checking and information verification: See a suspicious piece of information — paste it into Perplexity and let AI find the original source and assess its veracity.
  • Rapid academic and technical retrieval: By setting the Academic search focus, Perplexity searches only academic databases — quickly getting core conclusions from research papers and technical whitepapers.

💡 Tips

  • Always use Pro Search for complex questions: Standard mode is a single search; Pro Search performs multi-round deep search — for questions requiring multi-step reasoning, the quality difference is night and day.
  • Use Collections for systematic research: Add all queries on the same topic to a Collection and tell AI to use that Collection as context — subsequent answers improve in quality as knowledge accumulates.
  • Citations aren't gospel — click through and verify: While every sentence has a citation, the source might be untrustworthy content from a third-rate website. Critical thinking is always the last line of defense.

Overview

Perplexity was founded in 2022 in San Francisco by Aravind Srinivas (former OpenAI researcher), Denis Yarats, Johnny Ho, Andy Konwinski, and others. Positioned as an "answer engine," its goal is to use AI to directly answer user questions rather than returning a list of links like traditional search engines. It was also one of the pioneering products to introduce "cited sources" into AI responses.

2025 was Perplexity's fastest expansion year: it acquired enterprise search company Carbon in January, launched the AI browser Comet in June (made free for everyone starting October 2), and public reports indicate its valuation reached approximately $20 billion by September 2025. The product portfolio has expanded from search to browser, API (Sonar series), and enterprise editions, gradually evolving into an "AI information gateway."

Core Features

  • AI search with citations: Every factual answer includes numbered citations; click to jump to the original webpage for verification.
  • Pro Search deep retrieval: AI automatically generates multi-round follow-up queries, drilling deeper like a human researcher — ideal for complex research questions.
  • File upload analysis: Supports uploading PDFs, documents, and other files for AI summarization, Q&A, and key point extraction.
  • Spaces & Collections: Group multiple queries on the same topic into a space, building up context, also shareable with teams.
  • Model selection & focus modes: Switch between different underlying models, with support for academic, coding, finance, and other search focuses.
  • Comet browser & Sonar API: The AI browser handles multi-step web tasks; Sonar API is developer-facing with per-token billing.

User Experience

Register at perplexity.ai to start using it — no payment required. Type a question in the search box and get a structured, cited answer within seconds. You can ask follow-up questions or switch to Pro Search with a single click for deeper multi-round retrieval.

The typical workflow: throw complex questions at Pro Search, group scattered queries into the same Collection to build knowledge context, upload PDFs for long-form analysis. Daily fact-checking, material retrieval, and quick research can all be done in a single interface with almost no learning curve.

Pros & Limitations

Pros: Answers with citations, fully traceable, high information transparency; strong real-time capability covering the latest news; clean, ad-free interface with no SEO spam interference; Pro Search offers significant value for deep research.

Limitations: Citation source quality varies — AI cannot judge page authority; Chinese content indexing and understanding is weaker than English; for tasks requiring multi-step operations, it still falls short of dedicated Agent products; free tier has limited quotas.

Who It's For

  • Researchers, journalists, and content creators who need to quickly verify information;
  • Students and academics who frequently do material research and literature retrieval;
  • Professionals who regularly query industry information and competitor dynamics;
  • Developers wanting to integrate search capabilities (Sonar API).

Core Features

Blue River Technology's technical core is applying computer vision and deep learning to large-scale agricultural production. The company was founded in 2011 by Jorge Heraud and Lee Redden, whose backgrounds were in precision agriculture and robotics respectively. In 2017, John Deere — the world's largest agricultural machinery manufacturer — acquired Blue River for approximately $305 million, integrating its technology into Deere's agricultural equipment lineup.

Blue River has developed two core technology systems: See & Spray (visual recognition + precision spraying) and Sense & Decide (perception + decision-making). See & Spray is the commercialized product: it uses 36 high-speed cameras and an onboard GPU to identify every plant in the field in real time, applying targeted micro-doses of herbicide only to weeds. Sense & Decide is a more foundational AI decision framework that extends beyond weed control into seeding decisions, fertilizer optimization, and other broader precision agriculture scenarios.

From a technical architecture perspective, Blue River's system is a classic edge AI case — all image capture, AI inference, and spraying decisions happen in real time on the machine, without relying on cloud connectivity, ensuring real-time responsiveness and reliability in the field.

Hands-On Experience

Blue River Technology's technology is delivered to end users integrated into John Deere sprayers. When farmers work, the operating experience is basically the same as a traditional sprayer — just drive the tractor through the field while the system automatically handles recognition and spraying in the background. Drivers can view real-time recognition footage on the display in the cab: green bounding boxes indicate crops that have been excluded, while red bounding boxes indicate weeds that have been sprayed.

From a management standpoint, farm owners can view operational data on the John Deere Operations Center platform: coverage area per job, actual herbicide usage, chemical savings compared with broadcast spraying, weed distribution heatmaps, and more. This data is highly valuable for planning the next season's planting schedule and weed control strategy.

Pricing

Blue River Technology does not sell software or services independently. The See & Spray system is sold as an optional configuration on John Deere sprayers. Because it targets large commercial farms, total purchase costs are generally high, and specific prices vary by model, region, and configuration. Farmers interested in purchasing are advised to contact their local John Deere dealer for a quote.

Pros and Limitations

Pros: First, it significantly reduces herbicide use (saving about 66% according to John Deere data), delivering both economic and environmental benefits. Second, real-time processing is strong — operating speed is unaffected by AI inference latency and can run at normal field speeds. Third, thanks to John Deere's global service network, after-sales support and parts supply are guaranteed.

Limitations: First, the investment barrier is high — the equipment costs far more than traditional sprayers and only suits large-scale farms. Second, the technology is relatively closed — users cannot adjust the AI models themselves or export raw data to third-party platforms. Third, there is a certain scope of applicability regarding crop types and growth stages; it cannot deliver optimal results in every cultivation scenario.

FAQ

Q: What is the relationship between Blue River Technology and John Deere?
A: Blue River Technology was acquired by John Deere in 2017 for approximately $305 million. It is now a wholly owned subsidiary of John Deere, headquartered in Sunnyvale, California.

Q: What's the difference between See & Spray and conventional spraying?
A: Traditional spraying is broadcast — the entire field is treated whether or not weeds are present. See & Spray only sprays when weeds are detected, saving about 66% of herbicide.

Q: Does the system require a network connection?
A: Real-time field recognition and spraying do not require a network — everything is processed on the machine's local GPU. Syncing and viewing operational data requires a later network connection.

Q: Is it suitable for small farms?
A: From an economic return perspective, the equipment investment is high, so it currently suits commercial farms with larger acreage.

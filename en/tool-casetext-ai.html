Tool Overview

Casetext is an AI legal research platform for legal professionals, founded in 2013 by Jake Heller and headquartered in California, USA. Its flagship product, CoCounsel, is a benchmark product in the legal AI space – it was the world's first legal AI assistant built on GPT-4, specializing in legal research, contract review, and litigation strategy analysis. In June 2023, Thomson Reuters acquired Casetext for $650 million and integrated it into the Westlaw legal information ecosystem. In April 2025, the standalone Casetext brand was officially retired, with the product fully merged into Thomson Reuters' CoCounsel platform. CoCounsel now runs as Westlaw's AI extension module, delivering AI-driven legal workflow automation to law firms, corporate legal departments, and government legal agencies.

Core Features

Legal Research & Case Analysis: After a user describes a legal question in natural language, CoCounsel searches Westlaw's vast case database and generates memo-style answers with pinpoint citations. Unlike traditional keyword search, it understands analogical relationships between legal concepts – for example, when a user asks "under what circumstances are non-compete clauses unenforceable in California," the AI searches case law, statutes, and law review articles simultaneously. Every conclusion in the results comes with footnotes citing the original text directly, reducing the risk of the AI "fabricating cases" in legal contexts.

Contract Review: After uploading a contract, CoCounsel can automatically flag key clauses, highlight risk points, and suggest revisions based on preset review criteria (such as "check GDPR compliance" or "flag all indemnification clauses"). For batch contract review tasks (such as hundreds of supplier contracts in due diligence), CoCounsel can significantly reduce the time lawyers spend manually reviewing documents.

Deposition Summaries & Timeline Analysis: After uploading trial transcripts or deposition records, CoCounsel automatically extracts key facts, identifies relationships between people and the timeline, and organizes information chronologically or by topic. This feature is especially useful in complex litigation – lawyers can get a structured summary of key facts within minutes, replacing the "handwritten chronologies" that used to take junior associates days to compile.

Legal Memo Drafting: Given a legal question, CoCounsel can draft an initial legal memo with a four-part structure: "Fact Overview," "Legal Standards," "Case Analysis," and "Conclusions & Recommendations." All citations include footnote links, so lawyers can verify the original text in Westlaw with one click.

Hands-On Experience

CoCounsel is currently deeply tied to Westlaw – users must log in with a Westlaw account. After logging in, the interface is split into two columns: a conversation window on the left (similar to ChatGPT's interaction model) and a document preview/citation navigation area on the right. The workflow: enter a legal question (primarily in English) → the AI returns a structured answer → click a citation footnote to preview the original case text on the right → if unsatisfied, follow up or ask for a more detailed explanation. For contract review tasks, drag a PDF contract into the upload area, select a review criteria template (or customize), and the system returns annotated results in anywhere from tens of seconds to a few minutes. The learning curve is generally gentle – lawyers familiar with Westlaw typically get up to speed within half an hour. Note, however, that CoCounsel's support for Chinese legal content is very limited – the interface is currently English-only, and search and analysis focus primarily on the US federal and state legal systems.

Pricing

When Casetext operated independently, it was priced at $65/user/month (basic legal research). After the Thomson Reuters acquisition in 2025, CoCounsel is no longer sold standalone but is offered as an add-on module to Westlaw, requiring an additional fee on top of a Westlaw subscription. Since Thomson Reuters uses enterprise-level custom pricing, no specific prices are published on the official website; media reports estimate CoCounsel's add-on cost at around $100–300/user/month, depending on team size and the feature modules required. For small law firms and solo practitioners, this price barrier is significantly higher than the original standalone Casetext, which has driven some users to alternatives such as NexLaw and Harvey AI.

The prices above are reference information collected from public sources; actual prices are subject to real-time quotes on the official website.

Pros and Limitations

Pros: CoCounsel's accuracy and reliability in legal AI are industry-leading – because it is built on Westlaw's authoritative case database, every citation is traceable to its source, greatly reducing the AI "hallucination" problem. Contract review and deposition summary features directly replace repetitive work that previously required heavy manual effort, significantly impacting law firms' cost structures. Thomson Reuters' brand and compliance credentials also give large firms and enterprises peace of mind when adopting the product.

Limitations: prices have risen sharply and there is no longer a standalone subscription, effectively locking out small law firms and solo practitioners. Deep integration with Westlaw means users who do not use Westlaw cannot use CoCounsel at all. While its legal research capabilities are excellent, in legal writing (such as drafting complaints or defense briefs) it still falls short of human lawyers' depth of argumentation and strategic thinking. Support for non-US legal systems (including China) is extremely limited.

FAQ

Q: Can Casetext still be used standalone?
A: No. Casetext ceased standalone operations in April 2025; the product has been merged into Thomson Reuters' CoCounsel and must be used through a Westlaw subscription.

Q: Does CoCounsel support Chinese legal questions?
A: CoCounsel currently targets the US legal system; the interface is in English, and its ability to search and analyze Chinese legal content is extremely limited.

Q: Can CoCounsel's AI fabricate cases?
A: CoCounsel's citations all come from real cases in the Westlaw database, and every conclusion includes verifiable citation links. The "hallucination" risk is much lower than with open-source LLMs, but we still recommend manually verifying key citations.

Q: What alternatives are there for small law firms?
A: Alternatives such as NexLaw and LegesGPT are worth considering – they are friendlier on price and cover basic legal research and document analysis needs.

Tool Overview

Casetext is an AI legal research platform for legal professionals, founded by Jake Heller in 2013 and headquartered in California, USA. Its flagship product, CoCounsel, is a benchmark in legal AI — it is the world’s first legal AI assistant built on GPT-4, specializing in legal research, contract review, and litigation strategy analysis. In June 2023, Thomson Reuters acquired Casetext for $650 million, integrating it into the Westlaw legal information ecosystem. In April 2025, the standalone Casetext brand was officially discontinued, and the product was fully merged into Thomson Reuters’ CoCounsel platform. CoCounsel now runs as an AI extension module of Westlaw, providing AI-driven legal workflow automation for law firms, corporate legal departments, and government legal agencies.

Core Features

Legal research and case analysis: After users describe a legal question in natural language, CoCounsel searches relevant cases across Westlaw’s massive case database and generates memo-style answers with cited page numbers. Unlike traditional keyword search, it understands analogical relationships between legal concepts — for example, when a user asks “under what circumstances are non-compete clauses unenforceable in California,” the AI simultaneously searches case law, statutes, and law reviews. Every conclusion in the results comes with footnotes citing the original text, reducing the risk of AI “fabricating cases” in legal contexts.

Contract review: After uploading a contract, CoCounsel can automatically flag key clauses, highlight risk points, and suggest revisions based on preset review criteria (e.g., “check GDPR compliance” or “flag all indemnification clauses”). For batch contract reviews (such as hundreds of vendor contracts in due diligence), CoCounsel significantly reduces the time lawyers spend manually reviewing documents.

Deposition summaries and timeline analysis: After uploading hearing transcripts or deposition records, CoCounsel automatically extracts key facts, identifies relationships between people, and organizes information chronologically or by topic. This feature is especially useful in complex litigation — lawyers can get a structured summary of key facts within minutes, replacing the “handwritten chronologies” that used to take junior associates days to compile.

Legal memorandum drafting: Given a legal question, CoCounsel drafts a memorandum with a four-part structure: “Facts Overview,” “Legal Standards,” “Case Analysis,” and “Conclusion & Recommendations.” All citations include footnote links, and lawyers can verify the original text in Westlaw with one click.

Getting Started

CoCounsel is deeply integrated with Westlaw — users must log in with a Westlaw account. After login, the interface has two columns: a chat window on the left (similar to ChatGPT’s interaction model) and a document preview and citation navigation area on the right. The workflow: enter a legal question (primarily in English) → the AI returns a structured answer → click citation footnotes to preview the original case text on the right → ask follow-up questions or request more detailed explanations if unsatisfied. For contract review, drag a PDF contract into the upload area, choose a review criteria template (or customize one), and the system returns annotated results within seconds to minutes. The learning curve is gentle overall — lawyers familiar with Westlaw typically get up to speed within half an hour. However, CoCounsel’s support for Chinese legal content is very limited — the interface is English-only, and search and analysis primarily target US federal and state legal systems.

Pricing

Casetext was priced at $65/user/month (basic legal research) while operating independently. After the 2025 Thomson Reuters acquisition, CoCounsel is no longer sold separately but is offered as an add-on module to Westlaw, requiring an additional fee on top of a Westlaw subscription. Since Thomson Reuters uses enterprise-level custom pricing, no specific prices are published on the website; media reports estimate the CoCounsel add-on at $100–300/user/month, depending on team size and required modules. For small law firms and solo practitioners, this price barrier is significantly higher than the original standalone Casetext, causing some users to migrate to alternatives such as NexLaw and Harvey AI.

The prices above are reference information collected from public sources; actual pricing is subject to the official website’s real-time quotes.

Strengths & Limitations

Strengths: CoCounsel’s accuracy and reliability are industry-leading in legal AI — because it is built on Westlaw’s authoritative case database, every citation is traceable, greatly reducing the AI “hallucination” problem. Contract review and deposition summarization directly replace repetitive work that previously required significant manual effort, with a notable impact on law firm cost structures. Thomson Reuters’ brand and compliance credentials also remove security concerns for large firms and enterprises when adopting the product.

Limitations: Prices have risen sharply and standalone subscriptions are gone, effectively pricing out small firms and solo practitioners. Deep integration with Westlaw means users who do not use Westlaw cannot use CoCounsel at all. While legal research is excellent, it still falls short of human lawyers in legal writing (e.g., drafting complaints or defense briefs) in terms of argumentation depth and strategic thinking. Support for non-US legal systems (including China) is extremely limited.

FAQ

Q: Can Casetext still be used standalone?
A: No. Casetext ceased independent operations in April 2025; the product has been merged into Thomson Reuters’ CoCounsel and is accessed through a Westlaw subscription.

Q: Does CoCounsel support Chinese legal questions?
A: Currently CoCounsel primarily targets the US legal system; the interface is in English, and its ability to search and analyze Chinese legal content is extremely limited.

Q: Can CoCounsel’s AI fabricate cases?
A: CoCounsel’s citations all come from real cases in the Westlaw database, and every conclusion comes with verifiable citation links. The hallucination risk is far lower than with open-source LLMs, but manual verification of key citations is still recommended.

Q: What alternatives are there for small law firms?
A: Consider alternatives such as NexLaw and LegesGPT — they are more affordable and cover basic legal research and document analysis needs.

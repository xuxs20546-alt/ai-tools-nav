📖 Overview

Poe is an AI chat platform launched by Quora, whose core value is "one app, access to all major models". In Poe you can chat with dozens of AI models — ChatGPT (GPT-4), Claude 3 Opus, Gemini, Llama 3, Mixtral, Command R+, Stable Diffusion and more. The same question can be sent to multiple models at once for comparison. You don't need to register separate accounts or manage separate subscriptions on each model's official website — one Poe account is enough.

Poe's other innovation is the "bot creation and sharing" ecosystem: you can create your own custom bots by setting roles, knowledge, and instructions in prompts, then publish them for other Poe users to use. Today there are hundreds of thousands of user-created bots on Poe, covering everything from writing assistants and programming tutors to D&D game masters and mental health counselors. Poe also has a "Creator Monetization" program — if your bot becomes popular, you can earn income from it. The free tier provides a daily usage quota, and subscribing to Poe Premium unlocks unlimited access to all models.

✨ Pros & Cons

✅ Pros

  • Multi-model aggregation with comparison: send the same question to GPT-4 and Claude at the same time and compare answers — this "multi-verification" experience is very useful for key decision-making scenarios.
  • Rich, customizable bot ecosystem: create your own dedicated bots + use hundreds of thousands of community bots — from coding and poetry to psychological counseling.
  • Great cross-platform sync: Web, iOS, and Android seamlessly sync chat history; switching between devices never loses conversation context.

⚠️ Cons

  • Not always cheaper than direct subscriptions: Poe Premium at $20/month includes multiple models, but if you only use GPT-4 or a single model, going directly to the original platform may be cheaper.
  • Simplified interface features: advanced model features (like ChatGPT plugins or Claude Artifacts) may not be fully available in Poe.
  • Inconsistent bot quality: there are many community bots, but most are shallow prompt wrappers; truly high-quality bots require time and careful design.

🎯 Use Cases

  • AI model comparison and selection: not sure which model performs best on a specific task (coding, writing, translation)? Ask the same question to 4 models at once and compare directly.
  • Custom bots for specific scenarios: create a "product knowledge Q&A bot" for internal company use — team members can ask product-related questions directly through Poe.
  • Model explorers: want to experience the differences between AI models without registering one by one — try them all in one place on Poe.

💡 Usage Tips

  • Treat Poe as a "model launcher": use it to quickly determine which model suits a task, then go deep on that model's native platform once you've decided.
  • Spend time designing your custom bot's prompt: a great bot prompt should include role definition, task description, output format requirements, and a reasoning framework (e.g., "analyze from first principles first, then give a conclusion") — far better than hastily built community bots.
  • Be cautious with community bot outputs: you can't see a community bot's prompt — it may hide biases, false premises, or even harmful instructions.

🔧 Core Features

Poe's core capabilities revolve around "multi-model aggregation". First, it offers a unified chat interface where users can call mainstream models such as GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Llama 3.1, and Mistral Large within a single conversation — switching models doesn't require leaving the chat window, just click the model name. Second, Poe supports "multi-model comparison": after entering a question, users can send it to multiple models at once with one click and directly compare answer styles and content quality in a side-by-side view — extremely useful for tool selection or validating key decisions.

The third core feature is the bot creation and publishing ecosystem. Users define a bot's system instructions in natural language (including role, knowledge scope, output format, tone, etc.) to generate a customized AI assistant. Creators can choose to make bots public for other Poe users to use for free, or join the "Creator Monetization" program to earn revenue share based on bot usage. Poe also supports connecting bots to external APIs, letting developers wrap their own services as chatbots inside Poe. In addition, Poe has built-in image generation (integrating models like Stable Diffusion), so images can be generated and edited directly in the chat window without switching to other tools.

🖐 Getting Started

When first visiting Poe, users can register with an email address or a Google/Apple account. After registration, you enter the chat interface directly: on the left is the conversation list and the "Explore Bots" entry, on the right is the chat window. New users can click "Explore" to browse popular community bots across categories such as writing, programming, translation, and entertainment. Select a bot to start chatting — the experience is nearly identical to using ChatGPT or Claude.

Creating a custom bot is also straightforward: click "Create Bot" in the left menu, fill in the bot name and description, then describe the bot's behavior rules in natural language in the prompt box. For example, "You are a backend engineer proficient in Python; when answering code questions, provide time complexity and alternative solutions." After creation, you can test it immediately and publish it to the community once satisfied. From registration to having a usable custom bot, the whole process usually takes under ten minutes. Paid users can seamlessly sync chat history across iOS, Android, and Web — switching between phone and computer never loses context.

❓ FAQ

Q: Is Poe's free tier enough?
A: For light daily use (occasional questions, testing models), the free tier is sufficient. But if you frequently use advanced models like GPT-4 or Claude, the daily quota will run out quickly — consider upgrading to a paid plan.

Q: Are the models on Poe identical to the official versions?
A: Poe calls each model's official API, so core capabilities are basically the same. However, Poe may not open certain platform-specific features, such as the ChatGPT plugin store or full Claude Artifacts interaction. Core chat capabilities are essentially consistent with the official experience.

Q: How can I monetize the bots I create?
A: Poe has a creator monetization program. Bot creators can apply to join and earn revenue share based on bot usage. Specific rates and thresholds are detailed in Poe's official Creator Monetization terms.

Q: Does Poe support Chinese?
A: Poe's interface supports multiple languages (including Chinese), and most models handle Chinese input and output well. However, Chinese capability varies notably across models — Claude and GPT-4 generally produce better Chinese than open-source models like Llama.

Q: What's the difference between Poe and other multi-model platforms (such as You.com or HuggingChat)?
A: Poe's key difference lies in its bot ecosystem and monetization mechanism — it is not just an aggregation tool but a creator platform. You.com focuses more on AI search, while HuggingChat focuses on the open-source model community.

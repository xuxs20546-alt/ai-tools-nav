🔬 Core Features

AI-driven knowledge graph: BenevolentAI's core asset is a vast biomedical knowledge graph that structurally connects entities — genes, proteins, drugs, diseases, pathways — and their relationships from millions of research papers, patents, clinical trial data, genomics and chemistry data. The AI engine reasons over this knowledge graph to uncover cross-domain connections that human researchers would struggle to spot by reading alone — for example, an approved arthritis drug may intersect with the disease pathway of a rare condition.

Target identification and validation: the platform automatically extracts and ranks potential disease targets from massive biomedical data. The AI models weigh the strength of evidence linking a target to a disease, existing drug safety data, and druggability analysis to produce a multi-dimensionally scored candidate list for drug discovery teams to prioritize for validation.

New uses for approved drugs (drug repurposing): one of BenevolentAI's best-known achievements was early in the COVID-19 pandemic, when knowledge graph analysis identified Eli Lilly's rheumatoid arthritis drug baricitinib as potentially effective against COVID-19 — a prediction later confirmed in clinical trials and granted FDA emergency use authorization. This "new use for an old drug" model can dramatically shorten development timelines by building on existing safety data.

Precision medicine decision support: BenevolentAI is also exploring the clinical side — using AI to analyze patients' genomic and clinical data to provide data-driven recommendations for individualized treatment of complex diseases such as cancer.

🖐️ Getting Started

BenevolentAI is not a self-serve SaaS product for end users. The company operates in two directions: its own internal drug discovery pipeline, and research partnerships with pharmaceutical companies (such as AstraZeneca). For partners, BenevolentAI typically provides project-based platform access and collaborative research services, rather than an open platform anyone can sign up for.

In the partnership model, the pharma company's R&D team and BenevolentAI's scientific team jointly define research questions (e.g. "find new targets in XX disease area" or "validate the potential of XX compound in YY indication"), after which BenevolentAI scientists perform the analysis on their platform, with both sides communicating progress and findings regularly. This model lowers the technical barrier for partners, but also means partners have limited control over, and transparency into, the platform itself.

BenevolentAI also runs several proprietary drug pipelines (covering oncology, neurodegenerative diseases, immunology and more), some of which have reached clinical stage. For outside observers tracking its technology validation, the clinical progress of these proprietary pipelines is a core reference for judging the platform's real value.

❓ FAQ

Q: Is BenevolentAI a publicly listed company?
A: Yes. BenevolentAI is listed on Euronext Amsterdam under the ticker symbol BAI. As a public company, its financial position and R&D progress are disclosed to a certain degree.

Q: Is there any fundamental difference between AI-discovered drugs and those found by traditional methods?
A: No fundamental difference — AI is a tool that assists discovery by helping researchers screen and prioritize more efficiently across a larger possibility space. The final drug candidates still have to pass the same in vitro experiments, animal models, and clinical trials to verify safety and efficacy.

Q: Does the BenevolentAI platform offer an open API or academic access program?
A: Currently, BenevolentAI does not offer an open API or academic access program. The platform is primarily used through commercial collaborations and proprietary projects. Academic institutions interested in collaboration can inquire through the contact channels on the official website.

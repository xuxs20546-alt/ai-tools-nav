🔬 Core Features

Blue River Technology's core product is the See & Spray intelligent spraying system. Installed on John Deere's large agricultural sprayers, the system scans fields in real time using 36 high-speed cameras. The cameras capture imagery at a rate of 2,500 square feet per second, processing vast amounts of visual data every millisecond while the tractor travels at up to approximately 16 mph (about 25 km/h).

The built-in deep learning model can distinguish crops from weeds in real time. When a camera identifies a weed, the system precisely controls individual nozzles to deliver spot spraying within milliseconds; when crops or bare ground are detected, the nozzles shut off automatically and no spraying occurs. This "spray only where the weeds are" strategy fundamentally changes the traditional "blanket spraying" model. According to data published by John Deere, See & Spray technology can reduce herbicide use by about two-thirds, significantly lowering agricultural costs and environmental impact.

The system currently supports a range of major crops, including cotton, soybeans, corn, and more. See & Spray has now evolved to its second generation, further improving recognition accuracy and operating speed.

🖐️ Getting Started

Blue River Technology's product is not a software tool aimed at ordinary consumers — it is a hardware-plus-software system integrated into John Deere agricultural machinery. After farmers or agricultural enterprises purchase a John Deere sprayer equipped with the See & Spray system, it runs automatically during field operations. Operation is similar to a traditional sprayer: the driver does not need to manage complex AI settings — the system handles image capture, AI inference, and spraying decisions in the background automatically.

After a job is completed, the system generates an operation report showing the weed distribution heatmap identified during the job, the actual herbicide usage, and comparison data against blanket spraying. This data can be reviewed and analyzed through the John Deere Operations Center platform, helping farm owners optimize their subsequent planting and weed control strategies.

💰 Pricing

Blue River Technology's See & Spray system is not sold as standalone software — it is integrated into John Deere sprayer products. Pricing depends on the machine configuration selected. Because it targets large agricultural enterprises and growers of high-value cash crops, the See & Spray premium typically requires substantial acreage and high herbicide costs to deliver an economic return. 👍 Strengths and Limitations

Strengths: First, it significantly reduces herbicide use, lowering chemical costs and environmental impact; second, real-time recognition and spraying do not slow down normal operating speed; third, it integrates seamlessly with the John Deere agricultural machinery ecosystem, with data flowing into a unified farm management platform.

Limitations: First, the initial investment is high, making it suitable only for farms with sufficient acreage; second, recognition accuracy is affected by crop growth stage and weed density, and misjudgments can occur in some complex scenarios; third, it relies on John Deere's closed ecosystem, with maintenance and repairs limited to the authorized service network.

❓ FAQ

Q: Is Blue River Technology an independent company or part of John Deere?
A: Blue River Technology was acquired by John Deere in 2017 for approximately $305 million. It is now a wholly owned subsidiary of John Deere, headquartered in Sunnyvale, California.

Q: Can See & Spray identify all types of weeds?
A: The system is currently trained primarily on common broadleaf weeds and grasses. For rare weeds with unusual morphology, recognition accuracy may be lower. As more field data accumulates, the model's recognition capabilities continue to improve through iteration.

Q: Can individual consumers purchase it?
A: No. Blue River's products are aimed at commercial agricultural customers and are sold through John Deere dealers — they are not available at retail to individual consumers.

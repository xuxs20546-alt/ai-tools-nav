Overview

Locus (locus.sh) extends AI capabilities to the upstream of the supply chain — from warehouse sorting to intercity transport and last-mile delivery — forming a complete, intelligent fulfillment chain. The company was founded in 2015 in India by Nishith Rastogi and Geet Garg. It initially focused on location intelligence and later found its best application in logistics. Locus's supply chain solutions cover order allocation, in-warehouse picking routes, line-haul transport planning, and last-mile delivery. Its core value lies in using AI algorithms to connect previously siloed supply chain segments into one dynamically coordinated whole.

Unlike a pure Transportation Management System (TMS) or pure Warehouse Management System (WMS), Locus positions itself as a "supply chain orchestration layer" — an AI decision engine layered on top of existing ERP, WMS, and TMS systems that automates data flow and decision coordination between different systems.

Core Features

Smart order allocation: Based on inventory location, delivery address, capacity availability, and cost constraints, AI automatically decides which warehouse each order ships from and which route delivers it. The system supports order splitting — when an order contains multiple items stored in different warehouses, it automatically splits into multiple sub-orders and coordinates each fulfillment path.

Warehouse picking optimization: Locus's algorithms optimize picking paths and package sorting sequences within the warehouse based on destination distribution and delivery routes. For distribution centers using cross-docking, this feature can significantly reduce package dwell time in the warehouse.

Line-haul transport planning: For intercity and cross-region line-haul transport, Locus considers vehicle type (box truck, refrigerated, hazardous materials), load rate, driver hours, and road restrictions to provide loading plans and departure schedules.

Supply chain visibility: The dashboard provides end-to-end visual tracking from warehouse outbound to customer signature. Exceptions (delays, damage, rejections) are automatically flagged and trigger preset response workflows.

Getting Started

Locus's supply chain module is enterprise-grade SaaS and requires a pre-sales assessment and customized implementation before go-live. A typical implementation path: first connect the customer's Order Management System (OMS) and Warehouse Management System (WMS), import historical order and delivery data for AI model training, then run a 2–4 week pilot in a single city or region. After validating results, coverage is gradually expanded. Administrators monitor the operational status of each segment daily through the web console, and the system automatically flags exceptions that require human intervention.

Pricing

Locus's supply chain module uses enterprise custom pricing, with costs depending on order volume, the number of warehouses and routes covered, system integration complexity, and service level agreement (SLA) requirements. Strengths and Limitations

Strengths: Locus has deep experience applying AI to supply chain decisions. Especially with non-standardized addresses and complex road conditions in emerging markets, its geocoding and route optimization capabilities have a clear edge. The platform's integrated design (from warehouse to last mile) reduces the data silo problems created by patching together multiple systems.

Limitations: The product targets mid-to-large enterprises, and deployment cycles are long. For scenarios that only need a single function (such as pure route planning or pure WMS), Locus's comprehensiveness can become redundancy. In addition, its market presence and localization support in North America and Europe still have room to improve.

Who It's For

  • Third-party logistics providers managing mixed fleets (owned + outsourced)
  • Supply chain directors who need to connect warehousing, line-haul transport, and last-mile delivery across the full chain

FAQ

Q: What's the difference between Locus's supply chain module and a standalone TMS/WMS?
A: Locus is positioned as an "orchestration layer" rather than a replacement. It typically works alongside the customer's existing ERP/WMS/TMS, providing AI decisions and cross-segment coordination on top of multiple systems.

Q: How long does implementation take?
A: It varies by company size and system complexity; a typical cycle is 6–12 weeks. Smaller single-city deployments take about 4–6 weeks.

Q: How is data security ensured?
A: Locus is SOC 2 certified, supports private cloud and on-premises deployment, and all data transmission is encrypted.

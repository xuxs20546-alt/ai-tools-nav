Kimi Tips & Tricks: The Ultimate Weapon for Long-Document Processing

2025-04-28 · 7 min read · 🌙

Kimi's core weapon is not "reading long documents" but "reasoning based on long documents"

Many AI tools claim to support long context. You drag a PDF in, and it can summarize. But what if you need more than a summary? For example: "In this 200-page industry report, find all paragraphs mentioning the 'Southeast Asia market', map out how each country's market entry strategy evolved over time, analyze the disagreements between different authors, and then give me the 3 judgments you find most controversial." — Most AI tools fall apart right there.

Kimi's 2-million-character context window (roughly equivalent to the entire "Three-Body Problem" trilogy) is not valuable because it "can fit everything in", but because after fitting it all in, it can still perform deep cross-paragraph and cross-chapter reasoning. The following are real techniques I've accumulated from heavy daily use of Kimi — not a retelling of official case studies.

Tip 1: Upload an entire project folder at once and let Kimi do cross-analysis

Kimi supports uploading up to 50 files at once, each up to 100MB. In a single session you can upload PDF, Word, Excel, PPT, TXT, and images (text in images is extracted via OCR).

Killer scenario: Upload all materials for a project at once — requirements docs, competitive analysis, user interview records, market research reports — then ask:

"Combining all materials I've uploaded: is there corresponding verification in the user interviews for the weaknesses of Product A mentioned in the competitive analysis? Does the priority ranking in the requirements document align with the data in the market research report? Find all contradictions."

This kind of cross-file analysis is a unique Kimi capability. GPT-4o and Claude's context windows aren't large enough to hold this many files, and while Gemini has a large context too, its analysis depth on Chinese documents doesn't match Kimi's.

Tip 2: Read long documents in three steps — don't try to do it all at once

The most common mistake beginners make: upload a 300-page book and say "summarize it for me". The result is a three-level summary with zero information value — reading it is like not reading at all.

The right approach (three-step method):

  1. Structure mapping: "Please map out the table of contents of this document, noting the length share of each chapter, core themes, and the logical relationships between chapters." — This step builds your overall understanding.
  2. Targeted deep dive: Based on the results of step one, choose the parts you actually care about for deeper analysis. Don't have the AI analyze every chapter — that's a waste of time. Ask targeted questions only on sections strongly related to your needs.
  3. Cross-validation: "Does point A mentioned in chapter three get supplemented or contradicted in chapter six? Do the conclusions the author draws in the final chapter fully cover all the questions raised earlier?" — This step is what truly squeezes out the document's deep value.

Tip 3: Web search + document analysis = the strongest information synthesis

Kimi's web search and document analysis are not two separate features — they can work together in the same conversation.

Hands-on example:

  1. Upload a 2024 industry whitepaper
  2. Have Kimi extract the 10 core predictions from the whitepaper
  3. For each prediction, have Kimi search the web for the latest data: "Search 2025–2026 data and verify which of these 10 predictions have already come true, which have been falsified, and which are still evolving."

This is like using a static document as an anchor and dynamic search as validation — the output is a "prediction vs. reality" review report, far more valuable than reading an old report or a few news items on your own.

Tip 4: Use Kimi for initial review of contracts/legal documents (with prompt)

This is Kimi's most frequent business use case. Here's how:

"Please review the following contract and provide analysis along these dimensions:
1. [Risk clauses] List one by one all clauses unfavorable to Party A (us), including but not limited to: overly low liability compensation caps, unclear intellectual property ownership, unequal unilateral termination rights, unfavorable dispute resolution venue
2. [Missing clauses] Point out clauses a standard service contract should include but that are missing here
3. [Ambiguous clauses] Point out clauses that are unclear and open to different interpretations
4. [Revision suggestions] For each issue, give specific revision suggestions and negotiation talking points
5. [Priority ranking] Rank all issues from highest to lowest risk"

Note: Kimi's analysis results are initial-review references and cannot replace review by a professional lawyer. But it can help you filter out 90% of low-level issues before you hire a lawyer, so the lawyer's time goes where genuine professional judgment is needed.

Tip 5: Batch processing of academic literature and literature review writing

Upload 20–30 research paper PDFs and you can:

  • Automatically cluster by topic and output a classified literature matrix
  • Compare conclusions of different papers on the same research question
  • Identify citation relationships and academic lineages between papers
  • Draft an outline for a literature review based on these papers

Sample prompt:

"These papers all revolve around [research topic]. Please:
1. Group them by research method (quantitative/qualitative/mixed)
2. Within each group, sort by publication date and analyze the methodological evolution trend of that group
3. Extract the 3–5 most controversial academic viewpoints, noting which papers support/oppose each
4. Generate an outline for a literature review including introduction, method classification, controversy focus, research gaps, and future directions"

Kimi's limitations — know when not to use it

To be honest:

  • Not suitable for code generation and debugging: That's not Kimi's strength. Use Claude Sonnet/Cursor for coding, not Kimi.
  • Long multi-turn conversations lose early context: Although it supports a 2-million-character context, in actual multi-turn conversations, instruction-following quality declines after about 20 turns. If you need long collaboration, re-summarize the key points discussed every 10 turns or so.
  • Math reasoning is weaker than DeepSeek R1: For tasks requiring deep reasoning (mathematical proofs, complex logical reasoning), Kimi performs below dedicated reasoning models.
  • The free version has usage frequency limits: Free quotas for file uploads and web search are limited. Heavy users can subscribe to the paid version (currently about ¥50/month), which offers excellent value.

A Kimi prompt template worth saving

A universal template for when you throw a pile of documents at it and want high-quality analysis:

[Context] I am a [role], working on [task], and need to analyze these [quantity] [document types]
[Core questions] My top 3 questions are: [Q1], [Q2], [Q3]
[Analysis requirements]
- No generic summaries; every judgment must cite the original passage from the document
- If different documents contradict each other on the same question, point it out explicitly
- Present key comparative data in tables
- Give 3–5 conclusions you're confident about (mark confidence: high/medium/low)

Save this template. Every time you just change the [Context] and [Core questions] at the front, and analysis quality stays consistently high.

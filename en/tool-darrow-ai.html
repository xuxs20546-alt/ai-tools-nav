About Darrow AI

Darrow AI is a legal technology company founded in 2020, headquartered in Tel Aviv, Israel and New York, USA. Its core product is an AI-powered "Justice Intelligence Platform" that helps plaintiff law firms discover, evaluate, and manage mass tort litigation cases. In simple terms, Darrow is like a tireless legal investigator — it continuously scans real-world public data such as news, regulatory announcements, consumer complaints, and product recalls, using natural language processing and machine learning models to identify "potentially actionable harm events." It then automatically assesses the legal value of these events, predicts possible settlement amounts, and pushes the most valuable case leads to partner law firms.

Darrow's founder and CEO, Evyatar Ben Artzi, previously served in Israeli military intelligence. The team brings together technologists from Google and Microsoft as well as experienced litigators. The company's core technical innovation is transforming "case discovery" — a process traditionally dependent on lawyers' personal networks and experience — into a data-driven, standardized workflow. In May 2026, Darrow launched a brand-new platform for law firms that unifies case discovery, evaluation, settlement prediction, and case portfolio management into a single work interface, allowing firms to run their litigation practice like an investment portfolio.

To date, Darrow has raised tens of millions of dollars in venture funding and has established partnerships with top plaintiff law firms across the United States. Its platform has helped firms uncover multiple class action cases with significant social impact and commercial value in areas such as data privacy violations, defective medical products, and consumer fraud.

Core Features

1. Smart Case Discovery: This is Darrow's most essential capability. The platform's AI agents continuously monitor massive public data sources — from FDA adverse drug reaction reports and SEC corporate disclosures to state court litigation records and consumer protection complaint databases. When the AI identifies a pattern that could constitute a mass tort (for example, the same rare side effect appearing in multiple independent reports for a drug), it automatically generates a case lead report containing the event timeline, an estimate of potential victims, and relevant legal precedent citations. This effectively gives law firms a 7×24 intelligence analysis team.

2. Case Evaluation & Valuation: After a potential case is identified, Darrow's evaluation engine automatically analyzes historical settlements, trial outcomes, and litigation timelines for similar cases, then combines the current case's victim scale, evidence strength, and jurisdictional factors to generate an estimated "case value range." This feature lets lawyers quickly determine whether a case is worth taking on before committing significant time and resources.

3. Settlement Prediction Model: Trained on historical litigation data, Darrow's model can predict defendants' likely settlement willingness and amount ranges at different stages. The platform weighs factors such as the defendant's financial condition, past litigation record, and current media attention to produce a "settlement probability curve," enabling firms to build more data-supported negotiation strategies.

4. Portfolio Management: A core feature added in the 2026 platform. Firms can manage all active cases from a unified dashboard, monitoring each case's progress, resource investment, and expected returns. The platform's AI also offers optimization suggestions based on the firm's overall portfolio — for example, recommending increased investment in a particular legal area or flagging cases with weak evidence chains.

Getting Started

Darrow is a B2B platform built for law firms, and onboarding begins with "enterprise-grade registration." Firms first contact Darrow's sales team for a product demo and needs assessment, after which the platform is configured to the firm's size and practice areas. Once registration is complete, administrators can invite lawyers and case managers to the platform and set permissions for different roles.

After logging in, the main interface resembles a "command center" dashboard, with a navigation bar on the left and a case lead card feed in the center. Each lead card displays an event summary, a confidence score, an estimated victim count, and potential case value. Users can click any lead to open a detail page with the AI's full analysis report — including the evidence chain, regulatory citations, relevant precedents, and recommended actions. For experienced litigators, the quality of these reports is quite high, typically covering the equivalent of two to three days of junior associate research.

In the "case portfolio" view, firms can see the status of all active cases, upcoming milestone dates, resources invested, and expected ROI. Visually, this interface resembles portfolio management tools — each case is assigned a "health" score color-coded from green (on track) to red (needs attention). For large firms managing dozens of simultaneous litigation matters, this provides an unprecedented global view.

Strengths and Limitations

Strengths: First, its case discovery capability is unique — Darrow is one of the few platforms in legal tech today with "proactive case lead discovery" as its core capability, complementing rather than competing with traditional legal research tools such as Westlaw and LexisNexis. Second, its data source coverage is extensive — the public data the platform connects to far exceeds what a single attorney team could manually research, with a clear edge in the depth of analysis of specialized regulatory sources such as FDA and SEC. Third, the evaluation models are highly transparent — every case valuation report lists the underlying data and assumptions used by the model in detail, and attorneys can adjust parameters based on their own professional judgment.

Limitations: First, it is primarily suited to plaintiff-side litigation — its case discovery and evaluation logic inherently favors "finding potential liability of defendants," limiting its applicability for defense firms. Second, it depends on the quality and timeliness of public data — if some regulators' data updates are delayed or lack granularity, Darrow's analysis accuracy suffers. Third, the new platform was only released in 2026 — features such as portfolio management and end-to-end collaboration are still in early iteration, and their stability awaits long-term validation.

FAQ

Q: What's the difference between Darrow AI and LexisNexis?
A: LexisNexis is a traditional legal research tool — you give it a question and it finds answers among existing cases and statutes. Darrow, by contrast, proactively scans new data from the real world and tells you "here's a potential new case nobody knows about yet." The two are complementary, not substitutes.

Q: Can small firms or solo lawyers use it?
A: Darrow currently primarily serves law firms with dedicated litigation teams and has not yet launched a self-service plan for individual lawyers. Keep an eye on the official website for updates.

Q: How accurate is case evaluation?
A: Darrow has not published specific accuracy metrics for its models. It's worth noting that litigation outcomes are influenced by many unpredictable factors (judge variance, jury composition, etc.), so AI evaluation should serve only as a reference and cannot replace a lawyer's professional judgment.

Q: Which data sources does the platform support?
A: Darrow's data sources include, but are not limited to: the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System, the SEC EDGAR database, federal and state court electronic filing systems, the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau complaint database, and public content from news media and social platforms.

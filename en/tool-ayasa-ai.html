🔬 Core Features

AI-powered anti-money-laundering (AML) detection: Ayasdi's flagship product, Sensa AML, uses topological data analysis (TDA) and machine learning to identify money-laundering patterns in massive transaction datasets that traditional rule engines struggle to find. Traditional AML systems rely on manually written if-then rules and tend to generate a high volume of false positives, wasting compliance teams' review time. Ayasdi's AI approach can significantly reduce false-positive rates while improving detection sensitivity for new laundering techniques.

Financial fraud detection: Beyond AML, the Sensa platform also covers payment fraud, account takeover, and insider fraud. Through unsupervised learning, the system automatically discovers transactions that deviate from normal behavioral patterns — without needing to define in advance "what fraud looks like," which is crucial for keeping pace with constantly evolving fraud tactics.

Compliance automation: Ayasdi connects AI analysis results directly to compliance workflows. When the system flags a suspicious transaction or customer, it automatically generates a case report with a full evidence chain — including a timeline of related transactions, relationship network graphs, and risk assessments — and pushes it to compliance analysts for follow-up investigation. This dramatically reduces the manual work analysts spend on data collection and organization.

Network analysis & visualization: the platform provides interactive relationship network graphs that visualize hidden connections between seemingly unrelated individuals and entities. Compliance analysts can intuitively trace money-flow paths and understand the overall structure of a money-laundering network — not just isolated individual transactions.

🖐️ Getting Started

Ayasdi (now SymphonyAI Sensa) is an enterprise-grade platform for financial institutions, not a SaaS tool that individuals can sign up for and try on their own. A typical deployment process: SymphonyAI's solutions team engages with a bank's or financial institution's compliance department → data integration (connecting core banking systems and transaction databases) → model training and backtesting on historical data → training for compliance analysts → go-live. The full deployment cycle typically takes weeks to months.

For compliance analysts, Sensa's interface is built around case management. After logging in, analysts see a priority-ordered case queue (sorted by risk score from high to low). Each case includes an AI-generated risk summary, supporting evidence, and recommended actions — analysts simply review the AI's assessment and decide whether to escalate it into a suspicious activity report (SAR) or mark it as a false positive.

A hallmark of the system is explainability: every AI flag comes with an explanation of "why it was flagged," including which transaction features triggered the alert and how often these features appear in historical cases. For financial institutions that must explain their decisions to regulators, this transparency is a fundamental compliance requirement.

❓ FAQ

Q: What is the relationship between Ayasdi and SymphonyAI?
A: Ayasdi was acquired by SymphonyAI in 2019, and its core technology has since been integrated into SymphonyAI's financial-crime detection product line, "Sensa." The original brand "Ayasdi" has gradually faded, but the industry still often uses the name to refer to its TDA technology platform.

Q: What is TDA (topological data analysis), and how is it different from traditional machine learning?
A: Topological data analysis is a method that discovers patterns by analyzing the "shape" and "connectivity" between data points. Traditional ML tends to find linearly separable boundaries, whereas TDA is better at uncovering complex nonlinear relationships — such as circular money-laundering patterns hidden across multiple transaction rounds. This is the core differentiator of Ayasdi's technology.

Q: Will Sensa replace compliance analysts?
A: No. Sensa is positioned to augment compliance analysts, not replace them. AI handles surfacing suspicious cases from massive datasets and providing analytical support, while final judgment and decisions — especially reports submitted to regulators — remain with human analysts.

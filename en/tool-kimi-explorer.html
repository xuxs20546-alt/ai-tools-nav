📖 Overview

Kimi Explorer is a deep research feature launched by Moonshot AI in March 2025, built into the Kimi assistant. Unlike the standard Kimi's "one question, one answer" mode, Explorer's core design philosophy is "let AI work like a human researcher" — when you ask an open-ended research question, Kimi doesn't answer immediately but first autonomously plans its research path: it breaks the big question into sub-questions, searches for relevant information on the internet one by one, cross-validates the collected information, and finally synthesizes all findings into a structured research report. The entire process lasts about 3-10 minutes, during which users can watch Kimi's "thinking process" — what it is searching for, what information it found, and how it is reasoning.

Kimi Explorer's technical foundation is Moonshot AI's deep expertise in long-context processing — as early as 2024, Kimi attracted widespread industry attention for supporting a 2-million-character ultra-long context window (roughly the length of three volumes of The Three-Body Problem). Explorer combines this capability with autonomous search and multi-turn reasoning, allowing it to "remember" all retrieved information sources during the research and never miss details discovered earlier when generating the final report. Moonshot AI founder Yang Zhilin has said on multiple occasions that Explorer's goal is to become "everyone's personal research assistant," enabling people without information retrieval and analysis skills to produce a quality research report in a short time. As of mid-2026, Kimi's monthly active users have exceeded 30 million, with about 35% using the Explorer feature at least once a week.

✨ Pros & Cons

👍 Strengths

  • The ultra-long context window is the ideal carrier for deep research: the 2-million-token context capacity lets Kimi Explorer keep the full content of the 50-100 web pages retrieved during research in its "working memory," with no information truncation or forgotten early findings
  • The visualized research process builds user trust: unlike other AIs that output "black-box" answers directly, Kimi Explorer shows every search action and reasoning step in real time, so users can judge "whether it's heading in the right direction" at any moment, greatly reducing concerns about AI hallucinations
  • Output quality of Chinese long-form reports is unrivaled in China: thanks to Moonshot AI's heavy investment in Chinese long-document training data, Kimi Explorer's Chinese reports clearly outperform similar products in logical structure, argument depth, and language fluency

👎 Limitations

  • The research process takes time and cannot be interrupted midway: a full deep exploration takes about 3-10 minutes, and if users find the research direction is off, they can only wait for completion or manually abort and start over — no real-time fine-tuning of the direction
  • Weaker crawling of English and overseas websites: Explorer's search scope is mainly Chinese web pages; for research tasks requiring extensive citation of English primary sources (such as academic papers and foreign media reports), the breadth and depth of information coverage fall short of Perplexity's Pro search
  • Explorer usage is limited by your paid tier: free users can use exploration mode only 1-2 times per day, after which it automatically downgrades to standard conversation mode — a tight quota for users who need to run multiple research tasks in a row

🎯 Use Cases

  • Industry research and competitive analysis for professionals — a marketing manager needs to complete a research report on "the latest developments in the overseas market layout of China's new energy vehicles" within a week. After entering the topic in Kimi Explorer, the system automatically reviewed 60+ related reports and analysis articles in 8 minutes and produced a structured draft covering market data, strategy comparisons, and risk alerts.
  • Thesis topic exploration for undergraduates and graduate students — before finalizing a thesis topic, a student enters "research on the current state of LLM applications in medical diagnosis" in Kimi Explorer; the system autonomously searches domestic and international literature, identifies current research hotspots and gaps, and helps the student quickly lock onto a high-value topic direction.
  • Quick reconnaissance of new tracks for investors — when a VC analyst encounters an unfamiliar startup track (such as "AI protein folding"), entering the track name in Kimi Explorer provides a full industry landscape in minutes, including core technology routes, major players, funding status, and commercialization prospects.

💡 Usage Tips

  • Give Explorer a specific research question rather than a broad topic: entering "analyze the AI industry for me" yields a report that covers everything but lacks depth; if you instead enter "2024-2025 funding trends of Chinese AI pharma startups and comparison of leading companies' technology routes," Explorer's research direction becomes more focused and deeper
  • Treat the generated report as a draft, not a final version: although Explorer's reports are high quality, data accuracy and timeliness still require manual verification. It's recommended to spend 30 minutes checking key data and citation links after receiving the report, correct any deviations, and only then use it as official material
  • Add your expected report structure template at the end of your question: for example, "Please organize the report as follows: 1. Industry overview 2. Market size 3. Competitive landscape 4. Technology trends 5. Investment recommendations 6. Risk warnings" — Explorer will structure the content according to your preferences and output a report that better fits your needs

Core Features

1. Autonomous research path planning: after the user asks an open-ended research question, Explorer doesn't answer immediately but first breaks the question into 5-15 sub-questions and plans the keywords and information types to search for each one. This "plan first, then execute" workflow is fundamentally different from traditional AI's "answer directly" approach.

2. Multi-source information cross-validation: Explorer searches dozens of web pages in parallel (mostly Chinese pages), comparing and cross-validating information from different sources. If two sources give different numbers for the same data, Kimi flags the discrepancy in the report and tries to judge which source is more reliable.

3. Ultra-long-context research memory: based on Kimi's 2-million-token context window, Explorer can retain the full content of dozens of web pages retrieved during research, with no information truncation or lost early findings.

4. Automatic generation of structured reports: after research is complete, Explorer automatically generates a structured report containing heading hierarchy, data tables, cited sources, and summary recommendations. Users can continue asking follow-up questions or request expansions of specific sections.

Hands-on Experience

In Kimi's chat interface, users can click the "Explore" button above the input box to switch to Explorer mode. After switching, the system prompts "Enter a research question." A good research question should be open-ended and worth collecting information on (such as "funding trends and leading companies in China's AI pharma industry in 2025"), not a simple fact query (such as "what's the weather in Beijing today"). After submitting, a research progress panel appears on the right side of the interface, showing in real time the current search keywords, the number of pages visited, and the sub-questions being reasoned. The overall research structure resembles a "thought tree" — users can expand it to view the specific search content and reasoning process under each sub-question. When the research is complete, the full report appears in the main conversation area, where users can continue asking questions or request format adjustments.

FAQ

Q: What's the difference between Explorer mode and normal mode?
A: Normal mode is one question, one answer — suitable for daily chat and simple questions. Explorer mode autonomously plans research paths, searches multiple sources, and cross-validates — suitable for research tasks that require information gathering and comprehensive analysis.

Q: How long does one exploration take?
A: Usually between 3-10 minutes, depending on the complexity of the question and the amount of information to search.

Q: Are Explorer-generated reports trustworthy?
A: Report quality is high and citations are included, but data accuracy and timeliness still require manual verification. It's recommended to treat the report as a draft rather than a final version.

Q: How many explorations can free users do per day?
A: Free users get 1-2 per day; the exact quota may be adjusted with platform policy.

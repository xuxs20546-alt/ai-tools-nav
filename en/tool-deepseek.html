DeepSeek In-Depth Review

Open-source model with reasoning and coding on par with GPT-4

📖 Overview

DeepSeek was founded in 2023 by Hangzhou DeepSeek Artificial Intelligence Basic Technology Research Co., Ltd., backed by a deep learning research team from top universities including Tsinghua and Peking University, focused on efficient training and inference optimization of large language models. DeepSeek-V3, released at the end of 2024, adopted a revolutionary MoE (Mixture of Experts) architecture: despite a total of 671B parameters, only about 37B are activated per inference, and training cost was only about $5.57 million — less than one-tenth the training cost of comparable overseas models — sparking intense global debate in the AI community over the "efficient training paradigm." DeepSeek-R1, launched in early 2025, further added chain-of-thought reasoning, achieving 79.8% on the AIME 2024 math competition problems, on par with OpenAI o1's official release.

For Chinese users, DeepSeek's core appeal lies in: a completely free, ad-free web and app service; real-time display of its thinking process during conversations (like "watching AI solve a problem"); a natural advantage in understanding Chinese context and Chinese internet content; and fully open-source model weights — anyone can download the complete weights from Hugging Face for private deployment.

✨ Pros & Cons

👍 Pros

  • Math and coding reasoning in the world's top tier: DeepSeek-R1 scores near-perfect on authoritative math benchmarks such as MATH-500 and AIME 2024, and reaches the 90th percentile on Codeforces algorithmic problems — far beyond most free models and a formidable rival even to paid APIs
  • Transparent reasoning experience showing the full chain of thought: before answering, DeepSeek-R1 displays its entire reasoning process — including trial-and-error steps, self-doubt, and corrections. For learners, this is not just an answer but a lesson in problem-solving thinking, especially useful for tutoring elementary and middle school students in math olympiads and college students in advanced mathematics
  • Fully open source with local deployment support: model weights are fully open under the MIT license. Enterprises can download and privately deploy them on their own servers with data never leaving the intranet — a compliance advantage that API services cannot offer for strictly regulated industries such as finance and government
  • Chinese long-text generation quality ranks among the best of domestic models: on tasks requiring deep Chinese understanding and generation — Chinese scientific papers, policy document interpretation, classical poetry appreciation — DeepSeek outperforms most international rivals

👎 Cons

  • Multimodal capabilities clearly lagging: as of mid-2026, DeepSeek's web version does not support image upload or recognition, nor image/video generation. If you need to analyze charts or read text in images, you must first convert images into text descriptions
  • Unstable web search experience: although DeepSeek offers a web search feature, search source coverage is narrow (mainly Chinese web), and search results sometimes skip triggering model citations; users may need to retry manually
  • Slow response in reasoning mode: the R1 model generates a large number of intermediate reasoning tokens when displaying its full chain of thought; answering a complex math problem may take 60-90 seconds, which feels lengthy for fast-response, real-time conversations

🎯 Best Use Cases

  • AI tutor for advanced math and programming contests — throw a national high school math league problem or a LeetCode Hard problem at DeepSeek-R1: it doesn't just give the answer, it shows the entire journey from reading the problem → analyzing given conditions → trying solutions → verifying → spotting errors → adjusting approach → final solution. This transparent "chain of thought" is far more valuable for self-taught students of programming and math than just seeing the final answer.
  • Private AI deployment for enterprise knowledge bases — financial institutions or government agencies can download DeepSeek's open-source model and deploy it on internal servers, connecting it to their own regulations, business documents, and customer data to build an intelligent Q&A system that never leaves the intranet — enjoying the understanding of a large model while completely eliminating data leak risks.
  • Batch Chinese generation and translation of technical content — for open-source communities or tech blog teams that need to localize English technical documentation into Chinese at scale, DeepSeek excels at preserving code blocks and formatting integrity — in testing, converting a 120-page English Django tutorial to Chinese produced zero corrupted code examples and roughly 95% terminology translation accuracy.

💡 Usage Tips

  • Turn on "Deep Thinking" mode for math problems: below the input box there is a "Deep Thinking" toggle. For math, physics, and logic problems, be sure to enable it — it triggers R1's chain-of-thought mode, boosting accuracy about 30% over normal mode, at the cost of an extra 30-60 seconds
  • Use DeepSeek as a "second pair of eyes" for code review: paste your code along with the requirement description to DeepSeek and let it check for logic flaws, performance bottlenecks, and edge cases — many developers find its detection rate for low-level errors like memory leaks and null pointer exceptions in Python and C++ higher than manual review
  • Prefer quantized versions for private deployment: if you plan to run DeepSeek on your own server, choose the 4-bit or 8-bit quantized versions, which keep about 95% of reasoning quality while cutting VRAM requirements from 400GB to about 50GB — a single A100 80GB runs it smoothly

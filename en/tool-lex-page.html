Overview

Lex (lex.page) is an online word processor that deeply embeds AI into the writing workflow. Developed by Every, its design philosophy is "as simple as Google Docs, but with GPT-4- and Claude-level AI assistance built in." Unlike the traditional approach of copying and pasting between ChatGPT and a document, Lex makes AI part of the editor itself — you don't need to switch to another window to have AI generate content. Instead, you invoke AI right inside the document via hotkeys or slash commands to continue writing, rewrite, summarize, or brainstorm.

Lex's positioning clearly sets it apart from "all-in-one" tools like Notion AI or Jasper: it doesn't do project management, databases, or template marketplaces. It's simply a clean blank page plus an AI writing partner that's always on call. This minimalist design philosophy attracts users who want a distraction-free writing experience, including journalists, content creators, academic writers, and product managers.

Core Features

Built-in AI writing assistance: Lex's core differentiator is the deep integration of AI into the editor. Anywhere in a document, users can press a hotkey (Cmd+K or Ctrl+K) to summon the AI command palette and choose preset actions like "Continue writing", "Rewrite", "Summarize", or "Brainstorm" — or type a custom instruction. AI-generated content appears right at the cursor position, and users can accept it with one click or keep refining it.

Minimalist editor: Lex offers a clean, almost austere editing interface — a basic formatting toolbar at the top (bold, italic, headings, lists, etc.) and an infinite-scroll content area below. No sidebars, no pop-up ads, no superfluous UI elements. This design deliberately reduces visual noise and operational friction while writing.

Multi-model backend: Lex integrates multiple large models underneath, including GPT-4 and Claude. Users can pick their preferred model in settings or let the system assign one automatically based on task type. Different models do differ in writing style — GPT-4 excels at creative divergence and long-form continuation, while Claude is more careful and accurate in analytical writing and long-document editing.

Version history and collaboration: Lex keeps a complete edit history, so users can revert to any previous state at any time. The platform also supports basic collaboration, letting multiple people edit the same document simultaneously, similar to Google Docs' real-time collaboration.

Markdown and export: the editor natively supports Markdown shortcuts (e.g., # becomes a heading, * becomes a list) and offers export to Markdown, PDF, and plain text, making it easy to move content to other publishing platforms.

Getting Started

Lex has an extremely low learning curve. After signing up you land directly on a blank document, and the interface closely resembles Google Docs — anyone who has used an online document tool can start immediately. After writing a few paragraphs, when you get stuck, press Cmd+K and type "write the next paragraph about..." and the AI generates a continuation. This "hit a wall → hotkey → AI helps" loop feels very fluid and barely interrupts your writing rhythm.

In practice, Lex's most effective use isn't "having AI write an entire article from scratch," but "humans write the framework and core paragraphs while AI fills in details and transitions." For example, when writing a product launch blog post, the author drafts the title and topic sentences for each section, lets Lex generate drafts wherever more detail is needed, and finally polishes and fact-checks. This human-AI relay is noticeably faster than pure manual writing and more controllable in quality than pure AI generation.

One thing to note: Lex's AI assistance depends on network connectivity and the response speed of the backend models. During peak hours or with unstable connections, AI responses can lag by 3–5 seconds. Also, AI-generated content needs human fact-checking — it may confidently fabricate statistics or cite sources that don't exist.

Strengths and Limitations

Strengths: the depth of AI-editor integration is first-rate among similar products — no window switching or copy-pasting; AI assistance is fully embedded in the writing flow. The interface is minimal with zero learning cost and almost no distraction. The multi-model backend lets you pick a better-suited AI for the type of writing task. Solid version history means you never have to worry about AI ruining your original text.

Limitations: the feature scope is narrow — it's just a writing editor, with no knowledge management, no team wikis, and no databases. Teams that need to embed writing into a larger project management workflow may need to pair it with Notion or other tools. There's no localization optimization for Chinese prompts, so AI assistance for Chinese writing is less effective than for English. And there's no offline mode — all AI features require a network connection.

FAQ

Q: What's the difference between Lex's AI features and just writing with ChatGPT?
A: The core difference is the workflow. ChatGPT is a chatbot — you type a prompt in a chat window, it returns text, and you copy and paste it into your document. Lex embeds AI inside the editor, so generated content appears directly in your document, eliminating the friction of switching windows and copy-pasting. For writing tasks that rely heavily on AI assistance, this "zero-switch" experience adds up to a significant difference over time.

Q: Will Lex's generated text have an "AI flavor"?
A: AI-generated content does tend to have a certain "AI style" — overly neat sentence structures, a lack of personal voice, and possible factual errors. Lex's philosophy isn't to replace human writing but to help humans write faster. The best approach is "humans write the skeleton, AI fills the flesh, humans do the final review."

Q: Does Lex support writing in Chinese?
A: It supports a Chinese interface and Chinese input, but the AI models' assistance for Chinese writing isn't as strong as for English. The quality of Chinese prompt generation, the flow of continuations, and logical coherence still have room for improvement.

Q: Is the free plan enough?
A: The free plan offers a limited number of AI calls — it's good for experiencing the product logic and evaluating whether it fits your writing habits. If you want to use Lex as a daily writing tool, we recommend upgrading to the Pro plan.

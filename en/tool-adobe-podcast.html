Adobe Podcast In-Depth Review

Adobe's AI podcast production platform with AI noise reduction, voice enhancement, and online multitrack recording-to-text

📖 Tool Overview

Adobe Podcast is an AI podcast production platform officially launched by Adobe in 2023. Initially showcased at Adobe MAX under the codename Project Shasta, it was later opened to users worldwide as a free web-based tool. As a traditional creative software giant, Adobe's strategy for entering podcast production is very clever — it didn't try to reinvent a full DAW (Digital Audio Workstation), but instead focused on the three biggest pain points for podcast creators: poor recording quality, tedious post-editing, and the technical barriers of remote interviews. Adobe Podcast addresses each of these with AI technology, offering a free audio processing experience that sounds "like recording in a professional studio."

Adobe Podcast's core AI features include: Enhance Speech (voice enhancement), arguably the strongest free AI noise reduction and voice restoration tool on the market — upload a voice recorded in a noisy environment (such as a café or street), and the AI processes it within 10 seconds into clear audio approaching studio-microphone quality, removing background noise while rebuilding lost high-frequency detail, making the voice sound fuller and warmer; Mic Check (microphone check), which uses AI to analyze your recording environment and equipment and gives concrete improvement suggestions (such as "too far from the microphone, move about 15 cm closer" or "too much room reverb, consider adding sound-absorbing material"); and Studio mode, which supports multi-person remote recording with automatic sync, AI transcription, and fast text-based editing. All features run in the browser with no software installation required, and Adobe promises that free-tier users get the same feature set as the paid version (only export quality differs).

✨ Pros & Cons Analysis

👍 Pros

  • Enhance Speech noise reduction is stunning and free: in tests, uploading subway-recorded voice to Adobe Podcast nearly eliminated background noise, leaving clear, natural vocals with high-frequency detail (such as sibilants and breath) remarkably restored — noticeably better than manual noise reduction in Audacity
  • Mic Check is extremely valuable for beginners: new podcast creators often don't know what's wrong with their recording environment. Mic Check's AI analysis gives concrete advice on distance, angle, and room treatment — like having a free audio engineer tune your setup
  • Studio mode's text editing is incredibly practical: edit recordings like a document — delete a text passage and the corresponding audio segment is removed automatically. For creators who need to strip out lots of "ums" and "uhs", this is at least 5 times more efficient
  • Completely free with no ads: Adobe positions Podcast as a funnel product for the Adobe ecosystem, so there are no ads and no usage limits (the free version exports MP3 at 128kbps), making it extremely friendly for independent podcast creators on a budget

👎 Drawbacks

  • Lacks professional audio editing features: Adobe Podcast is not a full audio editor. You can't do multitrack mixing, add sound effects or background music, or perform fine EQ and compression. It works best as a first step for recording and post-production; finished material still needs to be exported to Audition or another DAW
  • Free-tier export quality is limited: the free version exports Enhance Speech-processed audio as 128kbps MP3, which isn't enough for podcasts with high audio quality demands (such as music or sound-design shows) — lossless export requires a paid subscription
  • Chinese speech enhancement is weaker than English: Enhance Speech's training data is primarily English, and while Chinese audio processing has improved, it's less effective than English and can occasionally introduce a slight "metallic" quality at high frequencies
  • Remote recording stability is average: Studio mode's multi-person remote recording relies on the WebRTC protocol, which can suffer from increased latency or audio-video desync on poor networks — less stable than competitors like Riverside.fm

🎯 Best Use Cases

  • Podcast recording and initial processing in non-professional environments — This is Adobe Podcast's strongest scenario. If you record podcasts at home without professional acoustic treatment, get optimization advice from Mic Check first, then run Enhance Speech after recording — your audio can go from "amateur-sounding" to "near-professional". Many independent podcasters use Adobe Podcast as the first step in their workflow.
  • A convenient recording solution for remote interviews — Studio mode lets you create a room link, and guests can join from a browser without registering or installing anything. Recording is automatically backed up both locally and in the cloud; after the interview, multitrack audio and AI transcripts sync automatically, saving the time spent on Zoom recording + manual transcription + alignment.
  • Quickly cleaning up voice tracks in videos — YouTubers often face wind noise and traffic noise when shooting outdoors. Export the video's audio track, process it in Adobe Podcast, then recombine the enhanced audio with the video — this dramatically improves the quality of outdoor vlogs.

💡 Usage Tips

  • Enhance Speech isn't magic — source recording quality still matters: AI enhancement has its limits — if the voice in the original recording is severely distorted or background noise completely drowns out the voice, the AI cannot create information that doesn't exist. Record in a relatively quiet environment; the AI is for polishing, not miracles
  • Text editing can drastically cut post-production time: AI transcription accuracy in Studio mode is about 95% for English and 85% for Chinese. Get into the habit of "checking the text first, deleting redundant passages, then exporting audio" — you can save 30-60 minutes of editing time per episode
  • As an entry point to the Adobe ecosystem, consider integration with Audition/Premiere: If you're already an Adobe subscriber, record and do initial processing in Adobe Podcast, export to Audition for fine mixing, then bring it into Premiere Pro to combine with video — the whole workflow connects seamlessly

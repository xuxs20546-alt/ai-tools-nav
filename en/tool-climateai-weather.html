Overview

ClimateAI (ClimateAi) is a technology company focused on applying artificial intelligence to climate risk forecasting, founded by Himanshu Gupta in San Francisco in 2017. Its core product is the ClimateLens platform — an enterprise-grade SaaS system that applies AI analysis to global climate data, satellite imagery, and weather models to provide customers in agriculture, food, and consumer goods with climate risk forecasts and adaptive decision recommendations over horizons from 1 day to 6 months. ClimateAI's investors include renowned VCs such as Radical Ventures, and its customers include major food companies and agricultural enterprises, helping them anticipate the impact of extreme weather events on crop yields and supply chains and formulate response strategies in advance. ClimateAI's core philosophy: climate change is not a distant threat but a present-day business risk — companies need AI-driven "climate intelligence" to protect their supply chains and assets.

Core Features

Climate Risk Forecasting (ClimateLens Forecast): ClimateLens uses AI models to analyze data from global meteorological agencies such as NOAA and ECMWF, combined with proprietary patented algorithms, to generate climate forecasts from 1 day to 6 months ahead. Unlike traditional weather forecasts, ClimateLens's predictions lean toward the "risk" dimension — not just "will it rain tomorrow," but "will the probability of extreme heat in a region over the next three months exceed your crop's tolerance threshold."

Crop Yield Modeling: For agricultural customers, ClimateLens combines climate forecasts with crop models to estimate expected yields for specific regions under different weather scenarios. For example, it can predict the decline in wheat yields in India's Punjab region if the monsoon is delayed by two weeks, helping food buyers adjust procurement strategies in advance.

Supply Chain Risk Mapping: Users can mark the locations of their suppliers, factories, or warehouses on a map, and ClimateLens continuously monitors climate risks at those locations — including extreme temperatures, floods, droughts, and hurricanes — and automatically alerts when risk levels rise.

Adaptive Planning Recommendations: ClimateLens doesn't just tell you "there's a risk"; it also provides actionable recommendations — such as "consider harvesting tomatoes in this region a week early to avoid yield losses from the forecast heat wave," or "rainfall in this region over the next three months is projected to be 30% below the historical average, so it's recommended to start backup water irrigation plans now."

Hands-On Experience

ClimateAI is a purely enterprise-grade B2B product with no free version or self-service trial for individual users. The typical adoption process: enterprise customers contact the sales team through the official website → requirements assessment and demo → a trial scope is defined (usually a specific region or crop) → ClimateAI's implementation team completes data integration and model configuration within 2-4 weeks → users view forecasts and risk dashboards through the ClimateLens web console. The console interface is map-centric, marking all monitored locations and risk levels (green/yellow/red), with a right-side panel showing detailed risk analysis and recommendations. Users can set custom alert rules and receive risk notifications via email or Slack.

Pros and Limitations

Pros: ClimateAI's professional depth in climate risk forecasting for agriculture and food supply chains is hard for competitors to match — its models are not generic "weather forecasts" but risk assessment engines optimized for specific crops and agricultural regions. The 6-month forecast window greatly extends corporate reaction time compared to traditional weather forecasts (typically 10-14 days). It has been validated by leading industry customers, and the product is quite mature. The backing of investors (such as Radical Ventures) also confirms its technological moat.

Limitations: Its purely enterprise-grade positioning means individual users and SMBs cannot use it — there is no self-service trial or pay-as-you-go option. Forecast accuracy begins to decline after 1-3 months, so long-range forecasts should be treated as reference rather than the sole basis for decisions. Platform coverage focuses mainly on agriculture and food; for other climate-risk-sensitive industries such as construction, insurance, or energy, feature depth may be insufficient. Pricing is opaque, and market education costs are high — convincing traditional agricultural enterprises to pay for "forecasts" requires a long sales cycle.

FAQ

Q: What's the difference between ClimateAI and personal weather apps?
A: ClimateAI is a professional platform for climate risk in enterprise supply chains. Its forecasting dimension is "what is the risk to your specific crops/products over the next 1-6 months," not just "will it rain tomorrow."

Q: Can ClimateAI be used in China?
A: ClimateAI covers global climate data, but data source access and localization in China depend on the latest information on the official website. Enterprises should contact the sales team to confirm data coverage for target regions.

Q: How accurate are ClimateAI's forecasts?
A: Accuracy is relatively high for the short term (1-4 weeks) and degrades noticeably at the 1-3 month horizon. Specific accuracy figures are not published on the official website and vary by region and climate type.

Q: Is there a free trial?
A: ClimateAI does not offer a public self-service free trial. Enterprises can contact the sales team to request a customized demo and proof of concept (PoC).

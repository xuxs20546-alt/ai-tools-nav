AudioShake In-Depth Review

A leader in AI stem separation, precisely splitting vocals, instruments, and drums from a mix

Overview

AudioShake was founded in 2020 as a company focused on AI source separation technology, co-founded by Grammy-winning audio engineers and machine learning experts. Unlike consumer-grade vocal separation tools, AudioShake set its sights on the professional audio industry from day one — it can break a finished mix down into individual stem files (vocals, drums, bass, guitar, keyboards, etc.), offering a completely different approach to music licensing, remix production, and film/TV sync licensing compared to traditional workflows. In 2023, AudioShake's Indie system (a deep learning model for separating individual instrument parts) placed in the top three across multiple metrics at the international Demixing Secrets source separation challenge, cementing its status as a technical benchmark.

AudioShake's core product is split into two modules: AudioShake Make (an online tool for independent musicians and producers) and AudioShake Pro (an API platform for labels, rights holders, and film/TV studios). Make supports uploading common formats such as MP3, WAV, and FLAC, and can output four separate tracks — drums, bass, vocals, and other instruments — within minutes, with online A/B listening comparison. Pro goes further, letting rights holders batch-process entire catalogs and automatically extract the vocal stem from each song for sync licensing negotiations — especially valuable because it doesn't compromise the integrity of the original mix. Traditional workflows require contacting the original recording studio to obtain multi-track files, a process that usually takes weeks or even months, and many older songs simply have no stems at all.

✨ Pros & Cons Analysis

👍 Pros

  • Industry-leading professional-grade separation quality: AudioShake's neural network is trained on tens of thousands of hours of professional studio recordings, achieving over 92% separation accuracy for vocals, bass, and kick drums. When handling complex pop arrangements in particular, residual bleed is noticeably lower than with similar tools
  • Batch processing and API integration: Labels can connect their entire catalog to the AudioShake Pro API and automate stem extraction for thousands of songs, greatly improving efficiency for rights management and secondary licensing
  • Unique lyric alignment and transcription features: Besides stem separation, AudioShake offers AI-driven lyric timeline alignment that pinpoints when each word appears in the lyrics, which is very useful for subtitle production and karaoke applications
  • Professional export formats: Supports lossless WAV 24-bit/48kHz export, meeting broadcast and film post-production standards without any quality loss from format compression

👎 Cons

  • Premium pricing aimed at enterprise users: The personal plan costs about $25 per month (limited to a certain number of tracks), while Pro requires contacting sales for custom quotes — a high barrier for independent musicians
  • Support for Chinese and Asian music is still being optimized: When processing Chinese pop songs, because Asian music makes up a smaller share of the training data, separation accuracy for certain Chinese folk instruments (such as erhu and pipa) is lower than for Western instruments
  • Processing speed varies with duration: While a typical 3-4 minute pop song takes only about 30 seconds, classical music or live recordings over 10 minutes can take 5-8 minutes, and a stable internet connection is required

🎯 Best Use Cases

  • Music rights sync licensing and sample cleanup — Film and TV production companies often need to license just the vocal or instrumental part of a song for a soundtrack, but traditional workflows require negotiating separately with multiple rights holders. AudioShake can extract the required stem directly from the full song, letting rights holders quote on that basis and dramatically shortening the licensing cycle.
  • Remix production for DJs and electronic musicians — Want to remix a classic hit but can't get the stems? Upload the original track to AudioShake, extract the vocal acapella and bassline, then rearrange them with your own beats and synths — this is one of the most common AudioShake use cases in electronic music today.
  • Ear training in music education — Music teachers can upload a symphony, extract the violin part, and let students listen and play along to it alone. Breaking complex polyphonic music down into single parts significantly helps students train their intonation and sense of rhythm.

💡 Tips & Recommendations

  • Upload the highest quality audio possible:AudioShake's separation quality is directly tied to the bitrate of the input audio. Upload at least 320kbps MP3 or lossless FLAC/WAV; 128kbps streaming quality will produce perceptible artifacts and a metallic sound after separation
  • Try Make first before deciding whether to upgrade to Pro:Individual users usually don't need Pro's batch processing. Process a few of your most important songs with Make first, confirm the separation quality, then decide whether a subscription is worth it
  • Pair with a DAW for secondary processing:No AI source separation is 100% perfect. After importing the stems into a DAW (such as Logic Pro or Ableton Live), use EQ and gate processors to clean up residual bleed and get results close to native multitracks

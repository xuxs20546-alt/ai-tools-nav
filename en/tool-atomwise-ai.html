🔬 Core Features

AtomNet deep learning drug screening: Atomwise's core technology is a convolutional neural network platform called AtomNet, purpose-built to predict binding affinity between small-molecule compounds and protein targets. Unlike traditional high-throughput screening (which physically tests millions of compounds one by one), AtomNet can virtually screen billions of molecules on a computer, dramatically improving hit rates while compressing the time and cost of early-stage screening to a fraction of conventional approaches.

Virtual compound library screening: The platform includes a vast virtual compound library (covering commercially available compounds, natural products, and synthesizable molecular space). Users only need to provide the target protein's structural information, and the system completes large-scale virtual screening within days, returning a ranked list of top candidate molecules. This capability is especially valuable for small and mid-sized biotech companies that lack wet-lab screening capacity.

Structural biology support: AtomNet doesn't rely solely on known protein crystal structures. For targets without experimentally resolved structures, the platform can screen using homology models and AI-predicted protein structures. This means many "undruggable" targets can also find new binding sites and candidate molecules through Atomwise's technology.

Collaborative R&D model: Atomwise works with pharmaceutical companies through collaborative R&D partnerships — it doesn't just sell software. Through technology licensing and joint drug discovery programs, it advances candidate drug pipelines together with partners including major pharma companies such as Pfizer and Eli Lilly.

🖐️ Getting Started

Atomwise is not a SaaS tool you can download and install yourself. Its services are delivered through formal research collaborations with pharma companies or academic institutions. A typical collaboration flow: the partner provides target information → the Atomwise team runs virtual screening with the AtomNet platform → Atomwise returns a ranked list of top candidate molecules with binding prediction data → the partner performs subsequent in vitro and in vivo validation in its own labs.

For academic institutions, Atomwise launched the "Artificial Intelligence Molecular Screen (AIMS)" academic collaboration program, through which researchers at hundreds of universities worldwide can access virtual screening services for free. Communication and project coordination are typically guided by Atomwise's scientific collaborations team. From submitting target information to receiving screening results, the cycle usually ranges from weeks to months, depending on project complexity and data quality.

Atomwise's advantage is that it places virtually no technical barrier on partners — they don't need expertise in AI or computational chemistry. But this model also means less flexibility: partners cannot adjust algorithm parameters themselves or view intermediate results in real time; the entire process is led by Atomwise.

❓ FAQ

Q: What's the difference between AtomNet and Schrodinger?
A: Schrodinger is a computational chemistry software suite based on physical principles (such as molecular dynamics and quantum mechanics) that users operate themselves. AtomNet is a deep learning-based virtual screening platform hosted and run by the Atomwise team. The former suits computational chemistry experts exploring on their own; the latter is more like an outsourced AI screening service.

Q: How accurate are Atomwise's virtual screening results?
A: According to Atomwise and third-party academic literature, AtomNet demonstrates notable hit-rate improvements across multiple target classes. However, any virtual screening result requires in vitro validation, and false positives and false negatives are inevitable. Peer-reviewed research published in Nature indicates the methodology is sound, but actual success rates vary by target.

Q: How can academic institutions apply for the AIMS program?
A: Submit an application through the academic collaboration page on Atomwise's official website, providing target information, research background, and expected goals. Shortlisted applicants are then connected with Atomwise's scientific team.

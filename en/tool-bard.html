Overview

Bard is a conversational AI chatbot that Google opened to the public in March 2023. It was initially built on the LaMDA model and later upgraded to PaLM 2 and the Gemini models. It was Google's direct answer to ChatGPT, and its biggest selling point was deep integration with the Google Search ecosystem: while answering questions, it could retrieve live web information and cite sources in real time, which was rare among comparable products at the time. In February 2024, Google renamed Bard to Gemini, unifying the brand and the models, and the old bard.google.com address now automatically redirects to Gemini. In other words, talking about "Bard" today is mostly talking about a piece of product history — the starting point of Google's all-in bet on generative AI. Understanding Bard's evolution also helps make sense of Google's strategic shift in the generative AI race: from cautious experimental releases to a full commitment to the Gemini brand.

Core Features

First, connected Q&A. Bard's answers did not rely only on the model's internal knowledge — it called Google Search for real-time information, and clickable source links appeared beneath the answers so users could verify them. This "search-enhanced" approach later carried over to Gemini. In the early days, this feature was especially well received, because it merged "chatting" and "fact-checking" into a single step.

Second, multi-turn conversation. It supported context-aware multi-turn exchanges: you could keep asking follow-ups and revising your requests on the same topic, which suited brainstorming, content drafting, and research organization.

Third, multilingual support. After launch, it gradually added support for many languages, including Chinese, and its Chinese answer quality was at a usable level compared with peers at the time.

Fourth, draft mode. Each answer came with multiple draft versions for the user to choose from — an interaction detail that set Bard apart from ChatGPT in its early days and made it easy to compare different phrasings.

Fifth, image capabilities. Later versions, based on Gemini models, could accept uploaded images and understand their content, and could generate images via Imagen. All of these capabilities were later folded into Gemini.

Getting Started

When Bard first launched, it used a waitlist. After Google I/O in May 2023, restrictions were gradually lifted and access expanded to more regions. The workflow was the same as mainstream chatbots: open the page, type a question in the input box, wait for the answer, and click "View other drafts" if you wanted to switch versions.

Typical uses included: asking Bard to outline the key points of a topic with source links; polishing a piece of copy through multi-turn conversation; comparing public information about several products, then following the links to verify further. Because answers cited search results, the information was more up-to-date than pure model-generated answers — the core reason users chose it back then.

One caveat: during the Bard era, model capabilities were still iterating rapidly, and early versions had factual errors that were publicly reported. Always verify important information against the original sources. Today the old entry point redirects to Gemini, and new users can simply use Gemini for the fuller feature set.

Pros and Cons

Pros: deep integration with Google Search, answers come with sources, and information stays timely; completely free with zero barrier to entry; the multi-draft design makes it easy to compare different phrasings; linked with the Google account system, with usage history synced across devices.

Cons: early model capabilities lagged noticeably behind ChatGPT, and factual errors were publicly discussed; the pace of feature updates was driven by Google's internal product strategy, and the brand went through several changes (Bard to Gemini), which could confuse users; availability in some countries and regions was restricted by policy; Bard itself has ceased operations and its legacy features are no longer maintained.

Who It's For

  • Users who need search-style Q&A with real-time information and source verification;
  • Practitioners and researchers who want to look back at the early product forms of generative AI;
  • Google ecosystem users who have moved to Gemini and want to understand its predecessor;
  • Product managers interested in the "search + conversation" product direction.

FAQ

Can I still use Bard? No. Since February 2024 it has been renamed Gemini, and the old URL automatically redirects to Gemini.

What's the difference between Bard and Gemini? Gemini is the renamed and upgraded version of Bard: the models moved from LaMDA/PaLM to the Gemini series, and capabilities such as image generation were added.

Did Bard cost anything back then? No. It was completely free for personal users during its availability period.

Which one should I use now? Just use Gemini (gemini.google.com) — it is the continuation and enhancement of Bard.

Can I recover my old Bard conversations? After the rebranding, data migrated to the Gemini system and the old Bard interface was taken offline. Check the data in your Google account for specifics.

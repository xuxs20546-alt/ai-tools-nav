Introduction

Buoy Health is an AI-powered online symptom analysis and care navigation tool incubated at Harvard Innovation Labs. Its core function is to help users understand the possible causes of their symptoms through a conversational interface and guide them toward appropriate care.

Specifically, Buoy's main features include: first, an AI Symptom Checker – users describe their symptoms in natural language (e.g., "headache for three days with nausea"), and the AI chatbot asks a series of progressive questions (e.g., "Is the pain constant or intermittent?" "Have you taken any medication?"), gradually narrowing down the possibilities and providing an initial analysis of possible causes; second, Care Navigation – based on the urgency of the symptoms and the likely causes, the AI recommends the appropriate level of care (self-care, booking a community clinic visit, or going to the emergency room immediately) and suggests nearby medical resources; third, health risk assessment – combining the user's age, sex, medical history, and other information, it provides a basic risk assessment for certain conditions.

Buoy's medical knowledge base is built on clinical data and medical literature from authoritative institutions such as Harvard Medical School, and has been validated by multiple academic studies. A study published in a journal in this field found that Buoy's triage recommendations showed high agreement with physicians' judgments for common conditions.

Getting Started

Using Buoy Health is very simple. After visiting the website or opening the app, users can start a symptom check without registering. The chat interface shows a welcome message: "Hi, I'm Buoy. What symptoms are you experiencing?" After users enter their symptoms, the AI asks questions step by step like a patient doctor – each question is multiple-choice or a short answer – and the whole process usually takes 3–5 minutes.

After the Q&A, Buoy generates a report: it lists several possible causes (ranked by probability), each with a brief explanation; it recommends a level of care (rest at home / see a community doctor / go to the emergency room); and it offers home-care suggestions. The report clearly states the disclaimer: "This tool does not provide medical diagnosis. If you have an emergency, call emergency services."

Pricing

Buoy Health's symptom checker is free for individual users. The platform's main business model targets enterprises and health insurers – through Buoy's employer health platform, companies can provide employees with intelligent care navigation, helping them choose appropriate care and thereby reducing medical spending for both employers and insurers. Pros and Limitations

Pros: First, its recommendations are based on authoritative medical data (Harvard Medical School), so the credibility of its AI advice is relatively high; second, the interaction feels natural – conversational symptom collection fits user habits better than filling out forms; third, the care navigation feature is practical – it tells you not only "what it might be" but also "what to do now," reducing unnecessary emergency visits.

Limitations: First, it is only a supportive tool and cannot replace a doctor's professional diagnosis – misjudgment is always possible; second, it currently covers mainly common symptoms and conditions, with limited ability to recognize rare diseases or atypical symptoms; third, it currently targets mainly the US market and English-speaking users, and Chinese-language support and the integration of localized medical resources are still incomplete.

FAQ

Q: Can Buoy Health replace seeing a doctor?
A: Absolutely not. Buoy is a supplementary reference tool that provides symptom analysis and care suggestions, not a medical diagnosis. If you have severe symptoms or an emergency, seek medical care immediately or call emergency services.

Q: Is Buoy's medical data source reliable?
A: Buoy is built on clinical data and medical literature from authoritative institutions such as Harvard Medical School and has been validated by peer-reviewed academic studies. However, AI advice can still be wrong and should not be treated as a final diagnosis.

Q: Do I need to register to use it?
A: The basic symptom checker can usually be used without registration. A personal account lets you save your query history and health profile.

Q: Does it support Chinese?
A: Buoy currently targets mainly the English-speaking market, so Chinese-language support may be limited. Please refer to the official website and app for the languages actually supported.

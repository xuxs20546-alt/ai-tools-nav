🔧 Core Features

Colourise (colourise.sg) is an AI tool dedicated to automatic colorization of black-and-white photos, developed by the data science and AI division of Singapore's Government Technology Agency (GovTech) and offered free to users worldwide as a public service. Unlike commercial photo colorization tools, Colourise positions itself as "bringing color back to historical photos," with unique value in old photo restoration and cultural heritage preservation.

AI automatic colorization engine: At its core, Colourise uses a colorization model trained on deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs). After a user uploads a black-and-white photo, the AI automatically analyzes objects, scenes, and texture features in the image, infers the most likely colors, and applies them — for example, recognizing the sky and filling it with blue, detecting grass and coloring it green, or identifying skin tones and adjusting them according to ambient lighting. The whole process runs in the browser, with no software to install.

Batch processing and high-resolution support: Free users can upload photos up to 2000×2000 pixels and process multiple images at once. This is especially useful for home users and archives that need to batch-colorize scanned old photo collections. Processing typically takes 10-30 seconds, depending on image resolution and server load.

Privacy-first design: As a government-led public service, Colourise is transparent about privacy: uploaded photos are automatically deleted from servers after processing and are never used for model training or commercial purposes. For users concerned about photo privacy, this is an important foundation of trust.

Scene recognition and color inference: Colourise's AI model was trained on massive datasets of color photos and can recognize hundreds of common scenes (city streets, rural fields, indoor portraits, natural landscapes, etc.), inferring sensible color palettes from scene characteristics. For example, it can distinguish a 1920s European street scene from a 1960s Asian market and produce colors consistent with the era and region.

🚀 Getting Started

The barrier to entry is extremely low: open colourise.sg — no registration or login needed — drag a black-and-white photo into the upload area, wait 10-30 seconds, and download the colorized version. The whole process takes under a minute, making it ideal for quick experiments.

The quality of the result depends largely on the original photo. For clear, high-contrast old photos with well-defined content (portraits, architecture, landscapes), Colourise performs admirably — natural skin-tone transitions, rich sky detail, and crisp textures in buildings. But for extremely blurry, badly faded, or overly complex images, the AI's inference accuracy drops, and colors may "bleed" into neighboring areas or certain objects may be misjudged.

One notable detail: Colourise offers no manual adjustment options. Unlike Photoshop's neural filters, you can't specify colors for particular regions or tune colorization intensity. It relies entirely on automatic AI judgment — great for "one-click" use cases, but potentially not flexible enough for professional restorers seeking precise control.

👍 Strengths & Limitations

Strengths

  • Completely free, no registration: as a government public service, Colourise has no commercial paid plans, and users don't need to provide personal information.
  • Colorization quality is top-tier among free tools: for historical photos with decent clarity, results look natural with high color fidelity — skin and sky elements are especially well handled.
  • Solid privacy design: uploaded photos are automatically deleted after processing and are never used for commercial purposes or model training.
  • Extremely easy to use: drag, drop, and done — no software installation or learning curve, suitable for users of all ages and skill levels.

Limitations

  • No manual color adjustment: fully dependent on automatic AI judgment, with no way to adjust or correct colors in specific areas — limited flexibility for professionals.
  • Resolution cap: the ~2000×2000 pixel limit may be insufficient for professional restoration requiring high-resolution print output.
  • Inconsistent on complex scenes: accuracy drops for extremely complex, badly faded, or very low-contrast photos.
  • Daily usage limits: as a free service there are daily request caps, so bulk batch processing of large photo sets may be restricted.

❓ FAQ

Q: Is Colourise really completely free?
A: Yes. Colourise is developed and operated by Singapore's Government Technology Agency (GovTech) and offered free as a public digital service. There are currently no paid plans and no hidden fees. That said, as a government project its operation depends on budget allocation, so long-term sustainability follows official announcements.

Q: Can Colourise's results match professional restorers?
A: No. Colourise is fully automatic AI colorization, suited to fast processing and everyday use. Professional restorers' manual colorization (hand-picked colors, layered grading) still wins on precision and artistic expression. But for most family old photos, Colourise's results are more than satisfactory.

Q: Are my photos safe after upload?
A: Colourise officially states that uploaded photos are automatically deleted from servers after processing and are never stored or used for model training. However, since data passes through servers, for photos containing extremely sensitive content we suggest assessing the risk and deciding for yourself.

Q: Does Colourise support video colorization?
A: No. Colourise currently only supports static photos (JPG, PNG). Video colorization requires frame-by-frame processing, with technical difficulty and compute demands far beyond what a free public service like Colourise can provide.
